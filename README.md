# Quantization Impact on the Accuracy and Communication Efficiency Trade-off in Federated Learning for Aerospace Predictive Maintenance

---

Federated learning for turbofan engine Remaining Useful Life (RUL) prediction,
with symmetric gradient quantization and FPGA deployment targeting.

Trains a custom lightweight 1-D CNN (AeroConv1D) across distributed edge clients
using FedAvg, evaluates the accuracy–communication trade-off of four quantization
levels (FP32 / INT8 / INT4 / INT2) under realistic Non-IID data partitioning on
the NASA C-MAPSS benchmark, and projects hardware feasibility on the Xilinx ZCU102.

---

## What it does

- **Federated training** — synchronous FedAvg across 10 clients, each holding a
  private subset of engine trajectories, with no data centralization.
- **Gradient quantization** — symmetric uniform quantization applied to weight
  *deltas* before aggregation, at 32 / 8 / 4 / 2 bits.
- **Non-IID evaluation** — engines are assigned to clients by trajectory (not
  shuffled), creating realistic label heterogeneity across clients.
- **IID vs Non-IID comparison** — baseline IID partition included to demonstrate
  the methodological bias of homogeneous client assignment.
- **Statistical analysis** — 10 random seeds, paired t-tests, coefficient of
  variation across seeds for every configuration.
- **FPGA projection** — analytical resource estimates (LUT, DSP, latency) for the
  Xilinx ZCU102 at each quantization level.

## Model: AeroConv1D

A purely feed-forward 1-D CNN designed to stay below 10k parameters
while remaining hardware-pipelinable on FPGAs. Recurrent layers (LSTM,
GRU) were deliberately avoided — their state accumulation amplifies
quantization noise across time steps and blocks deep hardware pipelining.

| Layer | Configuration | Output | Params |
|-------|--------------|--------|--------|
| Input | Window (50 steps, 14 sensors) | (B, 14, 50) | — |
| Conv1D | k=3, 14→32 + ReLU | (B, 32, 50) | 1,376 |
| MaxPool1D | k=2, s=2 | (B, 32, 25) | — |
| Conv1D | k=3, 32→64 + ReLU | (B, 64, 25) | 6,208 |
| AdaptiveAvgPool1D | → 1 | (B, 64, 1) | — |
| Linear + ReLU | 64→32 | (B, 32) | 2,080 |
| Linear | 32→1 | (B, 1) | 33 |
| **Total** | | | **9,697** |

The parameter count is verified via an assertion at simulation startup.

---

## Quantization scheme

Symmetric uniform quantization is applied per-layer to weight *deltas*
(not to the weights themselves during training):

```
Q_b(Δw) = (α / (2^(b-1) − 1)) · clamp(round((2^(b-1) − 1) · Δw / α), ...)
```

where `α = max|Δw|` per layer. This keeps local training at full precision
and only compresses the transmitted update.

| Level | Bits | Grid | Comm. cost |
|-------|------|------|------------|
| FP32 | 32 | continuous | 37.88 KiB/round |
| INT8 | 8 | 255 levels | 9.47 KiB/round |
| INT4 | 4 | 15 levels | 4.73 KiB/round |
| INT2 | 2 | {−1, 0, +1} | 2.37 KiB/round |

## Non-IID partition

Engines are assigned to clients by trajectory — each client receives
10 complete engine run-to-failure sequences, sampled without replacement
and without sorting by RUL. This means each client's RUL distribution
differs from the global distribution, reflecting heterogeneous fleet
operating conditions.

The IID baseline shuffles all training windows uniformly across clients,
erasing engine-level boundaries. This partition artificially suppresses
cross-client variance and overstates the benefit of any regularization
effect (including quantization noise).

Per-client mean RUL and Earth Mover's Distance from the global distribution
are exported to `results/noniid_stats_*.json` for inspection.

## FPGA projections

Targeting Xilinx ZCU102 (`xczu9eg-ffvb1156-2-e`):
274,080 LUT | 2,520 DSP | 912 BRAM36.

| Level | LUT | %LUT | DSP | %DSP | Latency | Fits |
|-------|-----|------|-----|------|---------|------|
| FP32 | 51,717 | 18.9% | 17,239 | 684% | 16 µs | No |
| INT8 | 12,929 | 4.7% | 4,309 | 171% | 4 µs | No |
| INT4 | 6,464 | 2.4% | 2,154 | 85.5% | 2 µs | **Yes** |
| INT2 | 3,232 | 1.2% | 1,077 | 42.7% | 1 µs | Yes |

DSP is the binding constraint. INT4 fits with 366 spare DSPs available
for an optional cryptographic co-processor. These are **analytical projections**
following the hls4ml scaling model, not validated on physical silicon.

## Key results

All metrics are mean ± std over 10 seeds, last FL round.

**FD001 — 1 operating condition**

| Level | MAE (cycles) | p vs FP32 | Score S (×10³) | p vs FP32 | CV_S |
|-------|-------------|-----------|----------------|-----------|------|
| FP32 | 17.52 ± 0.47 | — | 449 ± 123 | — | 27.3% |
| INT8 | 17.51 ± 0.48 | 0.520 | 447 ± 127 | 0.746 | 28.6% |
| INT4 | 17.48 ± 0.51 | 0.341 | 452 ± 115 | 0.802 | 25.3% |
| INT2 | 19.03 ± 1.62 | **0.018** | 800 ± 573 | 0.064 | 72.0% |

**FD002 — 6 operating conditions**

| Level | MAE (cycles) | p vs FP32 | Score S (×10³) | p vs FP32 | CV_S |
|-------|-------------|-----------|----------------|-----------|------|
| FP32 | 26.99 ± 1.69 | — | 923 ± 206 | — | 22.3% |
| INT8 | 27.24 ± 1.40 | 0.265 | 951 ± 167 | 0.364 | 16.9% |
| INT4 | 27.20 ± 1.84 | 0.264 | 938 ± 233 | 0.534 | 24.8% |
| INT2 | 21.53 ± 2.31 | **0.001** | 749 ± 347 | 0.207 | 45.8% |

INT4 achieves statistical parity with FP32 across all comparisons
(p > 0.05) while reducing communication by 8×.

INT2's lower MAE on FD002 is an over-regularization artefact — the
3-level {−1, 0, +1} grid prevents the model from adapting to the
heterogeneous 6-condition distribution, producing underfitting that
accidentally lowers MAE on some seeds. The NASA score coefficient of
variation (45.8%) confirms the model is operationally unreliable.

## Gradient-distortion privacy proxy

The quantity `L_priv = (1/|θ|) · ‖Δw − Q_b(Δw)‖²` measures mean
squared quantization distortion per parameter. Higher values raise the
noise floor for gradient-inversion attacks. This is an exploratory
indicator only — not a formal differential privacy guarantee.
FP32 has `L_priv = 0` by definition (no distortion).
